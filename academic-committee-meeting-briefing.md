## 2) arXiv Reading List — Curriculum Learning & AI in Education
1. **A Survey on Curriculum Learning** (arXiv:2010.13166) — Xin Wang, Yudong Chen, Wenwu Zhu. *Takeaway:* the field's reference framework — any curriculum reduces to a "Difficulty Measurer + Training Scheduler" pair, with automatic curricula split into self-paced, transfer-teacher, and RL-teacher families.
2. **Curriculum Learning: A Survey** (arXiv:2101.10382) — Petru Soviany, Radu Tudor Ionescu, Paolo Rota, Nicu Sebe. *Takeaway:* easy-to-hard training beats random shuffling with essentially no extra compute — a cheap, low-risk upgrade to argue for.
3. **Teacher-Student Curriculum Learning** (arXiv:1707.00183) — Tambet Matiisen, Avital Oliver, Taco Cohen, John Schulman. *Takeaway:* curricula generated automatically from learning-curve slopes matched or beat hand-crafted ones — precedent for automating course sequencing.
4. **Practical and Ethical Challenges of LLMs in Education: A Systematic Scoping Review** (arXiv:2303.13379) — Lixiang Yan, Lele Sha, Linxuan Zhao, et al. *Takeaway:* 53 LLM use cases across 9 categories plus the practical/ethical gaps (readiness, replicability, transparency, privacy) — the balanced-evidence paper for AI-in-the-classroom proposals.
5. **Fine-Tuning LLMs for Educational Support: Leveraging Gagne's Nine Events of Instruction for Lesson Planning** (arXiv:2503.09276) — Linzhao Jia, Changyong Qi, Yuang Wei, Han Sun, Xiaozhe Yang. *Takeaway:* a concrete recipe (CoT prompting + LoRA fine-tuning against official curriculum standards) for LLM-assisted lesson planning — directly usable for drafting this semester's teaching plan.

Optional 6th: *Towards responsible AI for education* (arXiv:2504.16148, Hooshyar et al.) — nine unresolved challenges and a hybrid human-AI route to trustworthy systems.
